## Overview

During my AI Internship at the University of Central Florida Center for Research in Computer Vision (May 2025, Aug 2025), I contributed to cutting-edge geolocalization research under **Rohit Gupta**, **Parth Kulkarni**, and **Dr. Mubarak Shah**.

Our core research project, **Geo-HiPO: Geolocalization via Hierarchical Preference Optimization of Multi-Modal LLMs**, focused on training multi-modal vision-language models (VLMs) to predict geographic place names and coordinates using preference-based learning grounded in real-world physical distances.

To support this research, I architected and deployed large-scale vision-language model training and inference infrastructure across multi-node GPU computing clusters.

## Technical Architecture

### 1. High Throughput Distributed GPU Sweeps
* **Infrastructure**: Executed batched PyTorch and CUDA jobs across multi-node V100 and H100 GPU clusters via SLURM.
* **Scale**: Scaled parallel deep learning inference workloads, running up to 50 concurrent, week-long evaluation sweeps to analyze edge-case model regressions and baseline drift.
* **Optimization**: Leveraged FlashAttention, PyTorch AMP, and DeepSpeed to maximize GPU compute efficiency, cutting experimental iteration timelines by weeks.

### 2. Autonomous Data Pipeline for 30M+ Images
* Automated high-throughput ingestion, filtering, and curation of over 30,000,000 geographic images.
* Built preference pair dataset generation tools using Hugging Face Transformers and TRL for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
* Optimized data streaming using custom multiprocessing data loaders, saving over 100 hours of processing time per dataset.

### 3. Empirical Evaluation & Error Tracking
* Engineered custom evaluation scripts and automated testing pipelines to systematically track Haversine distance error distributions (accuracy within a 25 km threshold).
* Authored technical reports and ablation studies comparing baseline vision backbones, quantization regimes (FP8/NF4), and prompt formatting structures.
