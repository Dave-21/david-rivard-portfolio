## The Problem

Low Earth Orbit satellites travel at about 17,000 miles per hour. From the ground, an overhead pass lasts only two to three minutes through a telescope lens.

To a camera sensor, a satellite looks like a tiny faint speck moving fast against a background of bright stars. Trying to aim and track that by hand is nearly impossible.

At Lake Superior State University (Aug 2024 - May 2025), I wrote software to calculate when satellites pass overhead, detect them in live camera video, and steer motorized telescope mounts to track them automatically.

---

## What I Built

* **120 FPS vision pipeline**: Wrote C++ and OpenCV code to process video feeds at 120 frames per second to keep up with fast-moving targets.
* **Target filtering**: Used frame differencing and background subtraction to remove stationary stars so only moving objects remain.
* **Object verification**: Tested YOLO models to confirm the moving target was a satellite rather than planes, clouds, or camera sensor noise.
* **Orbital math**: Used SGP4 orbit models and TLE satellite data to calculate the exact path and timing of each pass ahead of time.
* **Motor steering**: Wrote control software in C++ and Python over RS-232 serial cables to drive the motorized mount and keep targets centered.
* **Web dashboard**: Built a web interface in React and ASP.NET MVC to schedule passes, watch the live camera feed, and log tracking accuracy.
